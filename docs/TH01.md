# th01

Notes on how 東方靈異伝 ~ Highly Responsive to Prayers is structured and
how its parts map onto opentouhou. The overall design is in
[ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md).

## Source

The port follows the TH01 source reconstruction by nmlgc and the ReC98
contributors, as imported in
[N0zoM1z0/th01](https://github.com/N0zoM1z0/th01) at revision
`435222b6`.

The original is a PC-98 DOS game, written in 16-bit Borland C++ (Turbo
C++ 4) with master.lib. It is four programs: ZUNSOFT shows the logo, OP
the opening and menus, REIIDEN the gameplay and FUUIN the endings. They
hand the run's state to each other through a block that stays resident
in memory. Music and sound effects are MDRV2 data played on the OPNA
(YM2608). Graphics are two planar 640x400 pages of 16 colors from a
palette of 4-bit components, loaded from GRP (Pi), GRC, GRF, GRZ, BOS
and PTN files. Text comes from the PC-98's font ROM. The data sits in
packed archives.

## Programs

The port runs the four programs as scenes of one executable on
`shell/game` (`th01/main.c`): the logo, the opening (`th01/opening`),
gameplay (`th01/gameplay`), the score table and name entry
(`th01/score`) and the ending (`th01/ending`). `SESSION`
(`th01/session.h`) stands in for the resident block: settings, score,
lives, bombs, stage, route, continues, the random checkpoints and the
per-scene history that the ending reads.

- Start and Continue in the opening enter gameplay. Start clears the run
  history; Continue keeps stage, route, highest score and history.
- Game Over opens the score table, then the Continue menu.
- Final victory runs the route's ending, then the score table, then
  returns to the opening.

Gameplay covers seven scenes of five stages (`STAGE0.DAT` through
`STAGE6.DAT`) and the seven bosses: SinGyoku, then after the route
choice YuugenMagan, Elis and Sariel on the Makai route or Mima, Kikuri
and Konngara on the Jigoku route. Every scene loads before the first
tick; entering one selects its cached layouts and derives its
background and music names without reading files.

The original waits inside nested calls: a boss entrance, a palette fade
or a bonus count spins until enough VSyncs have passed. The port keeps
the call order and turns each wait into a phase that suspends its owner
and resumes on a later tick.

## Timing

The 24 kHz video mode the game runs in refreshes at about 56.42 Hz. The
rate comes from the GDC setup the game programs and the dot clock in
[DOSBox-X's timing code](https://github.com/joncampbell123/dosbox-x/blob/b6abbd5/src/hardware/vga_draw.cpp)
at revision `b6abbd5`. `th01/frame` spreads those VSyncs over the
shell's 60 Hz ticks: a VSync falls due less than one tick late, and an
integer phase keeps the long-term rate exact. Each program keeps its
own VSync interrupt counters, which advance once per VSync, including
inside nested waits. Millisecond delays become 60 Hz ticks with the
fraction carried over.

## Floating point

The original computes `double` expressions on the x87 with a 64-bit
significand and rounds only when it stores a result as binary64. Orb
gravity, missiles and Sariel's patterns depend on that. `th01/finite`
reproduces it with integer arithmetic, without the host's `long double`,
including converting to whole pixels straight from the 64-bit result.

## Random numbers

Gameplay randomness comes from master.lib's generator, a 32-bit linear
congruential state that gives 15 bits per step (`Math_Random`,
`th01/math.h`). The opening counts its frames, and Start or Continue
seeds the session's frame counter from that count. Gameplay advances the
counter once per frame and reseeds the generator from it at each life;
the ending seeds its draws from the counter at the last life exit.

## Rendering

`VIDEO` (`th01/video.h`) holds the two pages as one byte per pixel,
whose bits 0 through 3 are the blue, red, green and intensity planes,
and the 16-color palette. The game draws on one page and shows either;
the vertical scroll register becomes a row origin. Each tick the
presenter uploads changed pages as indexed textures, and the palette
when it changes, expanding each 4-bit component by 17.

Effects that combine planes work on the indices: the diagonal page
transitions, the EGC's wave restoration, the bomb's inverted and
scrolled pages, and the GRCG's single-color sprites. Some masks are not
in the data files: pellets, delay clouds, shootout lasers, the stage
bonus box, Mima's pillars and the page transitions load from
REIIDEN.EXE, whose length and SHA-256 `th01/reiiden_image` checks.

The PC-98's text layer lies over the graphics with eight fixed digital
colors, independent of the palette. `TRAM` (`th01/tram.h`) holds it as
a separate indexed layer with index 0 transparent, which the presenter
draws over the visible page during gameplay.

## Text

Text is drawn from the cells of a PC-98 font ROM: 8x16 cells for
halfwidth characters and 16x16 for fullwidth, as the original's CP932
bytes address them, so `\` draws the yen sign. th01 opens the first of
`FONT.ROM`, `NEC.BMP`, `ANEX86.BMP` and `FREECG98.BMP` in
`opentouhou/fonts/`, in DOSBox-X's order, and otherwise the built-in
Shinonome 16 (`core/text/font_rom.h`). The game turns every string it
draws into glyphs while loading (`th01/text`), then draws them on the
graphics pages or the text layer in the original's weights.

## Audio

Music is one MDT 2.3 object per track and sound effects are MDE 1.0
data. The readers follow the author's `MDRVDT.DOC`, `MD2MML.DOC` and
`EFP.C` in
[Mdrv2System 3.4f Rel2.31/1.23](https://www.vector.co.jp/soft/dos/art/se018677.html).
The shared `pc98` modules read them (`pc98/mdrv_data`), sequence them
as the MDRV98 3.4f driver does (`pc98/mdrv_seq`) and synthesize one
OPNA at 48000 Hz (`pc98/mdrv_synth`, `pc98/opna`). The chip model is
libvgm's YM2608 at revision `c5c03a2c`, in `src/pc98/opna/vendor/`.
Effect numbers stay one-based, as in the DOS driver.

th01 sets FM and rhythm balance offsets of eight. `th01/audio` scales
the synth's music and effects output by 1/65536, passes each through a
20 Hz DC blocker, and submits them to the Music and Effects groups of
one live input, so the two volume settings apply separately.

## Input

`TH01_INPUT` (`th01/input.h`) names the controls by role: the arrows
and the keypad directions separately, as the original senses them,
shot, strike, OK and cancel. Gameplay reacts to a change of a physical
source, so a keypad direction can reassert a direction already held on
the arrows. Two presses each of shot and strike, or shot with left and
right held, bomb. The menus, the ending and the logo's key press each
have their own sensing, as in the original.

## Scores and settings

The four score tables, one per difficulty, are kept in the original's
format (`REYHIES.DAT`, `REYHINO.DAT`, `REYHIHA.DAT`, `REYHILU.DAT`) in
the user directory. When a table is missing there, the original's file
is read as its starting content; the original files are never written.
A user file that does not read stays untouched and its table is not
saved.

The options (difficulty, music, bombs, lives) are saved in `config.ini`.
When the user directory has no `config.ini`, the original's 11-byte
`REIIDEN.CFG` is imported.

## Data files

The port reads `TOUHOU.DAT` for the logo's sprites, the `東方靈異.伝`
archive for everything else, and `REIIDEN.EXE` for the masks it embeds.
The archive's entries have complemented names, an XOR key of 76h and
optional run-length packing (`th01/pf.h`).
